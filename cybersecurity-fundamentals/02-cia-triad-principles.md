# 02 — CIA Triad and Security Principles

## Confidentiality
Information is accessible only to authorized people and systems. Controls include permissions, MFA, encryption, classification, and secure disposal. Example: only authorized HR staff can read payroll data.

## Integrity
Information remains accurate and complete and is protected against unauthorized changes. Controls include hashes, signatures, access restrictions, audit logs, and change approval. A matching hash shows two files match; it does not prove the source is trustworthy unless the reference hash is trusted.

## Availability
Authorized users can access systems and data when needed. Controls include backups, redundancy, capacity planning, patching, monitoring, and recovery plans.

## Other principles
- Authenticity: confidence that an identity or data source is genuine.
- Accountability: actions can be traced to an account or process through reliable records.
- Non-repudiation: evidence helps prevent a party from credibly denying an action, provided identity and keys are managed correctly.
- Privacy: personal information is handled fairly and only as needed.
- Least privilege: grant only the access needed for a task.
- Separation of duties: split critical tasks so one person cannot complete every sensitive step alone.

## Practical
For each scenario, identify the primary CIA property: unauthorized reading of a private file; changing a bank account number; a server outage; an unexpected change to a downloaded installer. Explain your reasoning. Use fictional scenarios, not other people's accounts.
