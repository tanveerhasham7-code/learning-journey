# 01 — Introduction to Cybersecurity

## Meaning
Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications, identities, and data from unauthorized access, misuse, damage, disruption, or theft. It combines people, processes, and technology. Antivirus alone is not enough: secure configuration, access control, updates, monitoring, backups, training, and incident response are also required.

## What do we protect?
An asset is something valuable: data, laptops, servers, routers, applications, user identities, business services, reputation, and operations. Asset owners determine business importance and acceptable risk.

## Why it matters
Incidents can cause financial loss, downtime, privacy harm, legal consequences, safety risks, and loss of trust.

## Main domains
- Network security: protects traffic and network infrastructure.
- Endpoint security: protects desktops, laptops, and servers.
- Application security: reduces software weaknesses.
- Identity security: protects accounts and permissions.
- Cloud security: protects cloud identities, workloads, configuration, and data.
- Data security: classifies, controls, encrypts, retains, and disposes of information.
- Security operations: monitors, detects, investigates, and responds.
- GRC: manages governance, risk, and compliance.

## Blue, red, and purple teams
Blue teams defend and monitor. Red teams conduct authorized simulations to test defenses. Purple teams improve collaboration. Penetration testing is permission-based and limited to an agreed scope.

## Basic workflow
Identify assets → understand threats and weaknesses → estimate risk → select controls → monitor → respond and improve.

## Practical
Create a small inventory of 3 non-sensitive assets on your own device. Record purpose, sensitivity, owner, and one protection requirement. Do not publish serial numbers, personal files, or secrets.
