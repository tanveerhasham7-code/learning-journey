# 09 — Governance, Risk and Compliance

## Governance
Defines who makes security decisions, who is accountable, how risk is accepted, and how performance is reviewed. Leadership remains accountable for business risk; technical teams operate controls.

## Policy, standard, procedure, guideline
- Policy: high-level mandatory direction (what and why).
- Standard: specific mandatory requirements.
- Procedure: ordered steps for a task.
- Guideline: recommended practice.

## Frameworks
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover.
- NIST SP 800-53: security and privacy control catalog.
- ISO/IEC 27001: requirements for an information security management system.
- CIS Critical Security Controls: prioritized safeguards.
- PCI DSS: requirements for environments handling payment card data.

Frameworks guide security work; they do not automatically certify an organization or guarantee security. Legal obligations depend on jurisdiction, sector, and data.

## Audit and evidence
Audits assess whether controls are suitably designed and operating, using approvals, configurations, logs, training records, and tests. Evidence should be accurate, dated, protected, and traceable.

## Practical
Write a one-page acceptable-use policy outline covering purpose, scope, permitted use, prohibited actions, account security, reporting, monitoring notice, and consequences. This is a learning exercise, not legal advice.
