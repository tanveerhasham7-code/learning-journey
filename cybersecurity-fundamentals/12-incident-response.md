# 12 — Incident Response and Recovery

An incident is an event that actually or potentially compromises confidentiality, integrity, availability, or violates security policy. A suspicious alert needs investigation before it is classified.

## Response lifecycle
- Preparation: plans, roles, tools, contacts, access, backups, exercises.
- Detection and analysis: validate reports, collect evidence, scope systems, determine severity.
- Containment: limit harm while preserving evidence and business operations.
- Eradication: remove the cause and exploited weaknesses.
- Recovery: restore services safely and monitor for recurrence.
- Lessons learned: document root causes, control gaps, and assigned improvements.

Follow the organization's approved plan and legal requirements.

## Evidence handling
Record who collected evidence, when, from which system, how, and where it was stored. Preserve originals where possible, restrict access, use hashes when appropriate, and avoid unnecessary system changes. Follow trained forensic procedures.

## Communication
Use approved escalation paths. Share facts, impact, actions, and uncertainty with appropriate people. Do not publish sensitive evidence or speculate.

## Practical
Tabletop scenario: a lab workstation shows an unexpected sign-in and suspicious process. Write initial questions, logs to request, who to notify, and possible containment. Do not execute anything harmful.
