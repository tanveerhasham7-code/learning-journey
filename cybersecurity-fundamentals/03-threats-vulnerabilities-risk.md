# 03 — Threats, Vulnerabilities, Exploits and Risk

- Threat: a potential cause of harm, such as a criminal, mistake, fire, or equipment failure.
- Threat actor: a person or group that may cause harm.
- Vulnerability: a weakness that could be exploited or triggered.
- Exploit: a method or code that takes advantage of a vulnerability.
- Attack vector: the route used to reach a target, such as email or a web application.
- Attack surface: all possible interaction points exposed to a threat.
- Risk: possibility of harm, considering likelihood and impact.
- Exposure: a condition that makes an asset accessible; exposure does not automatically mean compromise.

A vulnerability is a weakness; risk considers the environment and consequences. The same flaw may create different business risks on an isolated test device and an internet-facing payment server.

A simple qualitative model is Risk = Likelihood × Impact. This supports prioritization; it is not a universal precise measurement.

## Risk treatment
- Mitigate: reduce likelihood or impact.
- Avoid: stop the risky activity.
- Transfer/share: shift some financial consequences; responsibility is not eliminated.
- Accept: formally acknowledge remaining risk within approved limits.

## Example
Email phishing risk: users may be tricked, MFA may be absent, and sensitive mail may be exposed. Treatments include MFA, email filtering, user training, access restrictions, and sign-in monitoring.

## Practical
Create a risk register for three assets in your own lab. Record asset, threat, weakness, likelihood, impact, existing controls, proposed treatment, and owner. Use fictional labels before publishing.
